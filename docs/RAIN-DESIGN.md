# Rain beneath a roof window

The 1.4 rain model treats the display as a glass plane above the viewer. Drops approach nearly along the viewing direction, strike a particular point and deposit water there. The previous screen-height falling streaks are removed from the rain renderer. Snow keeps its own particle shader and movement.

Each incoming drop has a target, radius, duration and seed. A short perspective approach grows a soft, round image near that target. At contact the same event creates a brief, filled water lamella and triggers an optional quiet sound. The lamella spreads for about 55 ms, then recoils and fades into the deposited bead over about half a second. Larger contacts can leave a few tiny satellites; their volume is deducted from the central deposit. The wet pane retains and merges water, with a slight roof pitch allowing slow drainage.

Intensity changes the arrival rate of a Poisson process. It does not scale the width, length or speed of an existing drop. Drop sizes use the same distribution at every intensity. Wind affects the short approach drift. Most deposited water stays still. Ordinary runoff retains the 2.8-point-per-second limit; in 1.5, larger merged masses above 18 points in radius can accelerate gradually, up to 26 points per second, and leave through the lower edge.

Experimental water-on-glass work describes an initial spreading stage and subsequent surface behavior; that provides a qualitative reference for the contact sequence. See [Dynamics of water spreading on a glass surface](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021979704004588) and the [Drop Impact on a Solid Surface review and supplemental videos](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-fluid-122414-034401). This renderer uses deliberately readable timings and simplified optics. It does not solve fluid dynamics or reconstruct the photograph's depth or camera position.

Sixteen original damped taps are synthesized in memory. Visual contact events select a timbre and pan; intensity creates more contacts, rather than louder knocks. A bounded voice pool avoids restarting an active sound. Taps follow the ambience fade and are disabled immediately when sound or glass contact is switched off. One display supplies sound in multi-monitor sessions. The rain loop supplies diffuse background texture and has no baked-in close glass taps.

Only the app's own photograph is sampled for refraction. Desktop mode draws paired light/dark glass shading without screen capture. Turning glass off retains the short approach images and omits contact effects and collected water. Photograph softness remains bounded so the chosen view stays readable.

## Persistent water and the wiper (1.5)

Rain beads no longer have an expiry timer or an age-based opacity fade. Surface adhesion holds small beads in place, while coalescence increases a volume proxy proportional to radius cubed. The earlier 22-point merge cap is removed so it no longer discards volume. Larger water can overcome the pinning threshold and drain. The qualitative size-dependent release follows the distinction between retained and sliding drops discussed in [small drops on non-horizontal solid surfaces](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/on-the-ability-of-drops-or-bubbles-to-stick-to-nonhorizontal-surfaces-of-solids-part-2-small-drops-or-bubbles-having-contact-angles-of-arbitrary-size/C901DD1864AD2A6D3C02F28DD4AF839F). These are deliberately calm screen-space parameters, not measured SI units or a complete fluid solver.

The particle count remains bounded. On a fully populated wet pane, new water is absorbed by the nearest retained bead, preserving incoming volume instead of deleting an existing bead or ignoring the arrival. This is a dense-surface approximation. Narrow runoff trails still represent a thin residual film and fade; the actual water beads do not. Retention lasts within the current rain session; stopping the overlay or changing weather ends that pane.

A single-pivot beam wiper parks below the lower edge and makes a 2.35-second out-and-back stroke, with acceleration, deceleration and a brief reversal. Its shape is scaled to cover the display, rather than match a specific car model. The matte rubber lip, bevelled graphite spine, offset arm, hinge and shadow are procedural Metal geometry; there is no external 3D model or texture.

Every displayed frame tests water against the angular sector traversed by the actual blade, including bead radius. Water physics retains its fixed timestep, while the blade advances on each frame's elapsed time, including refresh intervals shorter than that physics step. Cleared water is collected into bounded bins on its leading edge and rendered as a travelling meniscus. It is discharged only when the blade passes below the visible boundary. Rain keeps arriving during both strokes. A trigger during an active cycle is ignored instead of snapping the blade back to its starting position. Ordinary desktop input remains click-through.

During a wipe, MetalKit requests the display's refresh rate up to 120 Hz, temporarily overriding the 30 fps economy setting; afterward, the weather returns to its selected rate. The request is a target, not a guarantee on every display. The hardware shader averages twelve positions across a short half-frame shutter interval (capped at 1/120 second), reducing temporal aliasing without blurring the photograph or retained water. Still rain/snow exteriors reuse their texture, mipmaps and bounded photo softening until photo, focus, dimming or display size changes; moving mist is redrawn each frame. See [Apple's frame-rate documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metalkit/mtkview/preferredframespersecond).

One accepted wipe event from the primary display starts an original quiet stereo wet-rubber sound with the same travel and reversal timing. It follows volume and mute, and stops immediately with the rain session or glass contact. The existing background beds and incoming-drop timbres are unchanged.

The configurable shortcut uses native Carbon hot-key registration without a keyboard event tap or Accessibility permission. A separate, focus-scoped local monitor captures the chosen combination only while the recorder button is active; the existing app-local shortcut dispatcher handles already assigned keys. Global bindings are suspended during capture and restored on completion, Escape, focus loss or popover closure. The replacement is checked before changing the saved binding; conflicts preserve the previous choice. Command or Control is required, and Sokak's pause/mute shortcuts are reserved. See [Apple's local event-monitor API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsevent/addlocalmonitorforevents(matching:handler:)).
